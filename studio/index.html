WHAT THIS EXPERIMENT CAN SAY

A useful model. Visible limits.

Phaseglass asks a precise question: which 3D point positions can explain this simulated complex radio signal under this declared model?

01 / FORWARD MODEL

One static point.

A transmitter-to-point-to-receiver path. Known antenna coordinates and frequencies. Baseline-isolated signal with optional constant complex gain per link. No walls, direct path, polarization, diffraction or human surface.

02 / BLIND INVERSE

Truth stays outside.

The inverse receives only the packet: geometry, frequencies, IQ, noise, bounds and the declared gain model. It searches a finite grid and refines promising starting points. Found candidates do not exhaust every possible location.

03 / IDENTIFIABILITY

Fit is not uniqueness.

Local sensitivity rank describes nearby constraints. It does not rule out a distant mirror position. A narrow frequency band or noisy signal can leave a family of compatible locations.

04 / INTERVENTION

Change one receiver.

A bounded search proposes a receiver coordinate to separate a selected competing pair. Apply regenerates a new simulated packet and reruns inference. The old observed IQ is never rewritten.

05 / FALSIFICATION

Counterexamples matter.

Extra scatterers and frequency-dependent hardware phase slopes are outside the inverse model. A mismatch can refuse a fit; a counterfeit phase slope can also fit well at the wrong location. Reveal simulated truth to test that assumption.

06 / SCALE & LIMITS

Simulation only.

The c/B quantity is a total-path delay scale, not a generic position accuracy claim. Search scores and acceptance bands are model-based heuristics, not probabilities. No hardware is required or supported.

Bounded work

Up to 4 transmitters, 4 receivers, 128 frequencies, 20,000 grid points and 40 local refinements. Coordinates and numeric inputs are validated by the server. Long operations can be cancelled.
